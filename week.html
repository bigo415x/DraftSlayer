172 Worthy at MIA START — WR1

  • Matchup: Miami is one of the more generous defenses against receivers.
  • Scheme: Their zone-heavy approach leaves the underneath space his motion/slot role thrives in.
  • Corners: First-year defensive coordinator, unproven room with no established starter — nobody there to punish a bad read.

Bottom line: real WR2 role (77% snaps). Clean start.

The QB List: FLEX (WR4) — agrees he's startable, grades it a hair below where I have him.

268 Adonai Mitchell NYJ at DET START — FLEX added this week

  • Matchup: Detroit is a fairly generous defense against receivers.
  • Scheme: Same deep-threat profile as Pierce and McLaurin, and Detroit doesn't have a strong coverage identity to take that away.
  • Corners: No standout corner on the roster — the unit was publicly criticized as disorganized last season.

Bottom line: his role just changed fast — 3 targets Wk1 → 12 targets Wk2, tied to Geno Smith taking over at QB. Matchup and role both point up — good add.

The QB List: FLEX (WR4) — agrees, same read on the target-share surge.

345 Jameson Williams vs. NYJ START — WR2

  • Matchup: The Jets are actually one of the more generous defenses against receivers so far this season.
  • Scheme: Their coach has a strong defensive backs pedigree, but it hasn't shown up in the results yet — the scheme concern doesn't really apply here.
  • Corners: Their top corner is solid, not elite — no reason to be scared of this matchup specifically.

Bottom line: the matchup's fine, his role isn't — snaps rose to 97% in Wk2 while his targets fell to 4. Weakest of the three starts, but still ranks ahead of the bench options.

The QB List: START (WR3) — more confident than I am; they're leaning on the matchup, I'm leaning on the target trend.

429 McLaurin vs. SEA BENCH

  • Matchup: Seattle is the toughest defense against receivers in the league right now.
  • Scheme: They play heavy zone with two safeties deep — exactly the look that shuts down a boundary receiver hunting a big play.
  • Corners: Their room is thin after their top corner left in free agency, which is the only real thing keeping this from being unplayable.

Bottom line: Jayden Daniels is out (dislocated elbow) and Marcus Mariota starts, stacking bad news on the league's toughest matchup. He'd have started most weeks this season, but with Worthy, Mitchell, and Jameson all grading out ahead of him now, this is the week to sit him — it's close (his Wk2 target trend was real), but not a clean call.

The QB List: START (WR3) — real disagreement here. They cite his 7 targets/game and red-zone role; I'm weighing the SEA matchup and Mariota downgrade more heavily. Your call if you'd rather ride the volume.

528 Malik Washington vs. KC BENCH added this week

  • Matchup: Kansas City is one of the stingiest defenses against receivers in the league.
  • Scheme: Their coordinator's disguise-heavy approach has been elite for years regardless of personnel turnover.
  • Corners: But they just traded away their top corner and are starting a rookie outside, and the slot spot specifically was still unsettled in camp — which matters, since Washington's whole game is underneath/slot volume, not outside routes.

Bottom line: the toughest matchup on your WRs this week, and Worthy/Mitchell/Jameson all rank ahead of him now that they're rostered. The role is real (98% then 73% snaps, 8 then 5 targets) — worth stashing and re-checking weekly — but bench him this week.

The QB List: SIT — agrees. "Talk to me when the quarterback completes more than 15 pass attempts a game."

RB matchup score pilot, week 3 · firm start/bench calls, ranked top to bottom

149 Bijan Robinson at GB START — RB1

  • Matchup: Green Bay's run defense grades out as average so far.
  • Scheme: They added size up front this offseason specifically to get tougher against backs like him.
  • Front seven: It's a new coordinator's first year installing that plan, so there's real uncertainty in how much it's actually working yet.

Bottom line: none of that matters — he's a true three-down back with a real receiving role (10 targets Wk1). Start him regardless; this is context, not a lever, for a genuine alpha.

The QB List: START (RB1) — no argument, "you're obviously starting Bijan Robinson."

218 Hubbard at CLE START — RB2

  • Matchup: Cleveland is one of the toughest run defenses in the league.
  • Scheme: His north-south running style runs right into a defense built to stop exactly that.
  • Front seven: Their linebacker duo (Schwesinger/Quincy Williams) is legitimately great even after trading away their star pass rusher.

Bottom line: lowest matchup score on your team, and I'm still starting him — he's your only other established every-week back, and neither Dobbins nor Brian Robinson has proven they can take the job. Bad matchup, still the right start.

326 Dobbins vs. LAR BENCH

  • Matchup: Below-average matchup, and it's trending worse.
  • Scheme: His explosive, cutback running style runs into a defense that just got a lot faster and more talented up front.
  • Front seven: The Rams' front got a real talent upgrade from the Myles Garrett trade — arguably a top-3 unit in the league now.

Bottom line: the role isn't delivering either — no receiving work, declining snaps either week. Bench him.

438 Brian Robinson at GB BENCH

  • Matchup: Same average Green Bay number as Bijan faces.
  • Scheme: His power, short-yardage running style runs right into the kind of front Green Bay built specifically to stop.
  • Front seven: Same first-year uncertainty as above.

Bottom line: lowest-ranked of your four, but the one to watch — his snap share nearly doubled (25%→48%) while Bijan's nearly halved. Bench this week, don't drop him.

The QB List: SIT — agrees, though their write-up for him is a known copy-paste of Bijan's blurb (a bug on their site, not new information).
